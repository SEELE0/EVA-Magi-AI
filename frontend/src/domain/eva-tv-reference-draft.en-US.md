# EVA TV and MAGI: Shared Background

Scope: the 26 episodes of the 1995–1996 Neon Genesis Evangelion TV series and the 1997 film The End of Evangelion (EoE). The Rebuild films, manga, and games are not incorporated into this narrative continuity. This document is a plot overview and a reference for the MAGI project, not a verbatim script, an official setting book, or quotations from characters. Episode headings are descriptive translations intended to help locate the material.

## Premise for Fictional Simulation

## Information Boundaries

- Distinguish events explicitly shown on screen, statements made by characters at the time, and later explanations or project inferences. Characters may conceal, misunderstand, or remain unaware of facts. When discussing an episode, do not give its characters knowledge of secrets revealed later.
- TV episodes 25–26 emphasize internal experiences during Instrumentality; EoE episodes 25′–26′ show more external events. Describe the two endings separately rather than forcing them into one definitive, undisputed shot-by-shot timeline.
- The MAGI project's three AI nodes, model calls, and voting interface are an interactive adaptation, not a complete representation of the original computer system. EVA supplies a fictional background; real-world issues should still be analyzed using verifiable real-world information.

## World, Characters, and Organizations

Second Impact changed the world. The surface defenses of Tokyo-3 and the underground NERV headquarters resist successive Angel attacks. Gendo Ikari leads NERV, Misato Katsuragi directs major operations, and Ritsuko Akagi oversees technical systems. SEELE controls resources behind the scenes and advances the Human Instrumentality Project. SEELE and Gendo cooperate publicly, but their objectives and methods do not fully coincide.

EVAs operate through synchronization with their pilots, but they are not ordinary machines. The relationships among pilots, EVAs, bodies, and souls unfold gradually. A.T. Fields act as barriers in battle and also connect to the later theme of boundaries between people. Instrumentality changes individual boundaries and consciousness; different factions have different intentions for its process and outcome.

## Character Relationships: Connections That Change with the Story

- **Shinji—Gendo—Yui Ikari:** Gendo is Shinji's father and NERV's commander; Yui is his mother. After Yui disappears, Gendo remains distant from Shinji until he needs a pilot for Unit-01. Shinji wants his father's approval but fears being valued only as a pilot. Occasional attempts at closeness do not make honest communication easy. Yui's connection to Unit-01 is a secret revealed later, unknown to Shinji early in the story.
- **Naoko Akagi—Gendo:** Naoko is Ritsuko's mother and MAGI's designer. After Yui disappears, Naoko and Gendo develop an intimate relationship beyond their work. Her feelings mean it should not be reduced to an unfulfilled crush or a relationship that has not begun. Gendo remains driven by Yui and his own plans; the story gives no simple answer about whether he reciprocates Naoko's feelings equally. In the episode 21 flashback, the young Rei repeats Gendo's disparaging description of Naoko. Naoko kills Rei and then dies by suicide. Her technical standing, emotional dependence, and pain at being dismissed collide here.
- **Ritsuko—Naoko—Gendo:** Ritsuko inherits her mother's technical work and MAGI, respecting Naoko while also feeling an enduring distance from her. As an adult, Ritsuko likewise has a professional and intimate relationship with Gendo, yet knows that holding his secrets does not equal earning his trust. Her relationship with Rei and the clones involves jealousy, guilt, and disappointment in Gendo. In episode 23, she exposes and destroys the clone facility and breaks with him. Do not treat mother and daughter as identical or reduce both relationships to acting solely for love.
- **Misato—Shinji / Asuka:** Misato is both an operations commander and an adult caring for the two teenagers. She houses and supports Shinji while also requiring him to fight. Her relationship with Asuka is that of an adult and a teenager living together; she may not notice Asuka's deterioration in time. Care, authority, and duty coexist. Obedience in the chain of command does not imply intimacy or trust.
- **Shinji—Rei—Gendo:** Shinji and Rei begin as strangers and grow closer through the battles and mutual protection of episodes 05–06. Rei's special trust in Gendo leaves Shinji curious and confused. As she gains experience, she starts making her own choices. Her identity and connection to Yui emerge later; early versions of these characters should not know those secrets.
- **Shinji—Asuka:** They are peers, pilots, and, for a time, housemates. Their synchronization training in episode 09 shows that they can cooperate. Competition over ability, the desire for recognition, and different ways of expressing themselves repeatedly create friction. Moments of closeness do not erase their loneliness. Attraction, tentative approaches, and harm coexist; do not assume they are a stable couple or simply enemies.
- **Asuka—Rei:** Asuka uses achievement and visibility to assert her existence, while Rei often appears quiet and obedient. Both are evaluated within NERV's pilot system but experience its pressure differently. Asuka's dislike and rivalry toward Rei intensify without erasing either person's individual circumstances.
- **Shinji—Toji / Kensuke:** Toji initially blames Shinji after his sister is injured in battle, but becomes his friend after seeing what piloting entails. Kensuke is interested in EVA and also forms a school friendship with Shinji. In episodes 17–18, Toji is selected as a pilot without Shinji knowing. This information gap draws their friendship into NERV's orders and concealment.
- **Shinji—Kaworu:** Kaworu appears only in episode 24, yet quickly gains Shinji's trust through direct acceptance. After repeatedly losing support, Shinji especially needs this understanding. Kaworu then reveals his identity as an Angel and leaves Shinji with a painful choice. Do not move this brief, intimate relationship into earlier episodes or assign one definitive label to every expression of feeling.
- **Misato—Kaji—Ritsuko:** Misato and Kaji were lovers and become close again after reuniting. Kaji's investigation of NERV entwines personal feelings with intelligence work. Misato and Ritsuko are longtime acquaintances and coworkers, but Ritsuko knows far more technical secrets. Genuine concern exists alongside concealment, conflicting duties, and increasingly divergent positions.

When writing, distinguish established intimate relationships, one-sided feelings, and viewers' inferences about motives. Respect each episode's information boundaries.

## MAGI in the Original Work

MAGI is a three-part supercomputer designed by Ritsuko's mother, Naoko Akagi: MELCHIOR, BALTHASAR, and CASPER. It performs calculations and administrative tasks for NERV and embodies different facets of Naoko's personality. “Scientist / mother / woman” is a common description of this design. The precise correspondence between those facets and the three computers still needs to be checked against scenes; it is not asserted here as an absolute rule.

In TV episode 13, an Angel enters the facilities and MAGI in microscopic form, controls two parts, and tries to take the third to trigger headquarters' self-destruction. Ritsuko uses the remaining system to counter it. In EoE 25′, Ritsuko attempts to destroy headquarters through MAGI, but CASPER refuses. The two incidents involve different permissions and thresholds; they do not establish that every original MAGI decision uses a two-out-of-three majority.

## TV Episode Summaries

The “Issues” for each episode are project analysis labels, not original dialogue or official conclusions.

### Episode 01 | The Angel Arrives

Shinji is summoned to Tokyo-3 by the father he has not seen for years and almost immediately encounters an Angel attack. He meets NERV's underground facilities, Gendo, and Rei Ayanami, the injured pilot of Unit-00. Gendo demands that he pilot Unit-01 immediately. Unprepared for EVA, Shinji decides amid fear and reluctance to let Rei be hurt further. There is no period of adjustment: the organization's urgent demands fall directly on a teenager.

**Issues:** Emergency orders, consent by minors, the father–son relationship, and the source of duty.

### Episode 02 | The Unfamiliar Ceiling

Shinji wakes in hospital with incomplete memories of the battle. He later recalls Unit-01 going berserk after he lost control and killing the Angel. Misato takes him into her home. Being rescued does not resolve his uncertainty about piloting EVA, his father, or his own worth. Unit-01's power also appears, for the first time, unlike a weapon fully controlled by its pilot and command system.

**Issues:** Responsibility after loss of control, post-battle care, and EVA autonomy.

### Episode 03 | The Phone That Never Rang

Shinji starts school and meets Toji and Kensuke, learning that Toji's sister was injured in the previous battle. During a new Angel attack, Toji and Kensuke enter the battle zone. Shinji takes them into the cockpit and continues fighting under extreme pressure. Seeing the pilot's circumstances changes Toji's resentment. Battles take place within a city, and every “victory” has concrete civilian costs.

**Issues:** External costs of combat, observers' perspectives, obedience and on-site judgment.

### Episode 04 | Rain, After Running Away

Shinji leaves Misato's home under the strain of repeated battles and relationships. Wandering near the city's outskirts, he briefly encounters life without combat duties but cannot simply return to it. After NERV retrieves him, he and Misato must confront whether he will continue piloting. His eventual return does not solve the problem; both are willing to keep building a relationship before they have all the answers.

**Issues:** The right to leave, conflict between care and orders, and the hedgehog's dilemma.

### Episode 05 | Rei, Beyond Her Heart

Shinji is puzzled by Gendo's closeness to Rei. During an earlier Unit-00 activation experiment, Gendo rushed into danger to rescue her, unlike the father Shinji knows. Shinji tries to communicate with Rei but understands little of how she sees herself and Gendo. Unit-00 is successfully reactivated, and a new Angel then severely damages Unit-01 with a long-range energy attack.

**Issues:** Observed facts versus inferred motives, and the agency of a quiet person.

### Episode 06 | The Decisive Battle of Tokyo-3

Facing an Angel that counterattacks automatically and advances toward headquarters, Misato arranges a long-range sniping operation. Shinji operates the positron cannon while Rei shields him in Unit-00. The operation also requires nationwide power and extensive logistical support. When the first shot fails, Rei blocks the counterattack and gives Shinji a second chance. Afterwards, they share a sincere, awkward exchange. Victory relies on mutual protection rather than individual heroism.

**Issues:** Coordinated operations, shared risk, and trust through action.

### Episode 07 | Something Made by Humans

Another organization demonstrates JA, a nuclear-powered giant robot intended as an alternative to EVA. The public demonstration becomes a runaway crisis, and Misato enters the machine at great risk to stop it. Afterwards, it emerges that someone within NERV manipulated the competitor's failure. Technical merit, control over plans, the ability to cause an accident, and responsibility for its consequences become inseparable.

**Issues:** Conflicts of interest, institutional competition, and manipulation in the name of safety.

### Episode 08 | Asuka Arrives in Japan

Asuka and Unit-02 reach Japan with a fleet, accompanied by Kaji. An Angel attack at sea prompts Asuka to demonstrate her abilities but forces her and Shinji to pilot together under unfamiliar conditions. Misato defeats the Angel with a risky tactic combining the fleet and EVA. Kaji later delivers an important object to Gendo. Asuka enters Shinji's class and daily life, beginning the relationship among the three pilots.

**Issues:** A newcomer's self-assertion, improvised cooperation, and a public mission with secret cargo.

### Episode 09 | Hearts and Minds in Sync

An Angel splits into two coordinated parts when struck. Shinji and Asuka cannot win separately and must live and train together until their movements are almost perfectly synchronized. Their friction persists, but a battle demanding precise cooperation gives them a shared rhythm. Coordination does not require agreement; it requires acknowledging the other person's existence and being willing to adjust.

**Issues:** Synchronization and independence, training costs, and joint decisions.

### Episode 10 | Diving into Lava

NERV discovers an immature Angel inside a volcano and initially attempts to capture it alive. Asuka descends into the lava, but the objective shifts from retrieval to destruction as the Angel develops. Specialized equipment, environmental pressure, and a changing enemy defeat the original plan. Improvised technical measures and Shinji's rescue allow her to escape. The sample-collection goal exposes NERV's research ambitions alongside its defense obligations.

**Issues:** Changing objectives, exploratory risk, and rescue priorities.

### Episode 11 | In the Still Darkness

A major power failure disables automation at NERV while an Angel approaches. Command staff and pilots are separated and must rely on manual operations, limited communications, and the teenagers' own actions to bring EVAs into battle. Infrastructure normally hidden behind the system becomes visible. The organization continues through specific people and procedures rather than perfect control on a screen.

**Issues:** Power-loss response, single points of failure, and manual takeover.

### Episode 12 | The Value of a Miracle

An Angel falls toward the city from high altitude. Misato orders all three EVAs to intercept it with A.T. Fields at the predicted impact point. The plan has a very low chance of success, but there is no time for a safer alternative. Afterwards, Shinji receives rare praise from Gendo and realizes he may have been piloting for that approval all along. A large-scale defense operation intersects with one person's emotional needs.

**Issues:** Low-probability decisions, commanders' responsibility for risk, and rewards changing motives.

### Episode 13 | The Angel Invades

An Angel enters NERV's test facilities in microscopic form, adapts to its surroundings, and invades MAGI. It controls MELCHIOR and BALTHASAR and tries to seize CASPER to trigger headquarters' self-destruction. Ritsuko faces an accelerating contest for control, not an enemy EVA can repel. She uses the remaining system to introduce a counter-program and force the Angel to destroy itself. Afterwards, she explains MAGI's connection to Naoko, making the technical system visibly a family inheritance.

**Issues:** Adversarial input, self-destruction permissions, personality modeling, and emergency human intervention.

### Episode 14 | SEELE, the Throne of Souls

The first half revisits Angel incidents and shows the framework of Gendo's reports to SEELE: frontline combat is part of a larger plan. The second half tests pilot–EVA interchangeability. Unit-00 again loses control, and its intended target remains unclear; Misato and Ritsuko interpret it differently. Rei's internal monologue also suggests that her self-understanding cannot be reduced to obedience.

**Issues:** Selective reporting, experimental anomalies, and judgment with insufficient evidence.

### Episode 15 | Lies and Silence

A wedding, a grave visit, dates, and everyday conversations briefly shift relationships away from battle. Shinji and Gendo meet at Yui's grave. Asuka tests her closeness to Shinji. Misato and Kaji renew their relationship as she becomes more involved in his investigation. Kaji shows her the giant being sealed beneath NERV and offers his explanation at the time. The point is not that every mystery is solved, but that characters begin realizing earlier explanations were unreliable.

**Issues:** Information asymmetry, tentative intimacy, and provisional conclusions.

### Episode 16 | A Sickness Unto Death, and Then…

Improved synchronization makes Shinji more willing to attack, after which Unit-01 is swallowed into another space through an Angel's shadow. Outside, Misato and Ritsuko disagree over rescue costs and command authority. Inside, Shinji confronts questions about who he is. Unit-01 violently breaks free. The rescue succeeds but deepens fears that the EVA cannot be controlled.

**Issues:** The trapped person's subjective experience, rescue costs, and new risks after success.

### Episode 17 | The Fourth Candidate

An EVA experiment goes wrong at the American branch, and Unit-03 is transferred to Japan. NERV selects Toji as its pilot, but Shinji does not know his classmate will enter the next test. School life continues while institutional procedures push an ordinary teenager toward the frontline. The information gap underlying the next tragedy is established: who decides, who bears the consequences, and who is kept unaware.

**Issues:** Pilot selection, the scope of informed knowledge, and transferred risk.

### Episode 18 | Choosing Life

An Angel takes over Unit-03 during activation, turning it into a threat to NERV. Shinji knows a pilot is inside and refuses to attack. Gendo uses the Dummy System to take over Unit-01 and destroy Unit-03. The pilot survives, but Shinji learns it was Toji. Mission completion and moral injury coexist: a machine carrying out violence its pilot refused does not remove anyone's responsibility.

**Issues:** Refusing orders, automated takeover, and harm to allies or civilians.

### Episode 19 | A Man's Battle

Shinji decides to leave after the incident involving Toji. A stronger Angel then breaches the defenses and defeats Units-02 and 00. After speaking with Kaji, Shinji chooses to return to Unit-01. Its energy runs out in battle, yet it awakens on its own and devours the Angel, acquiring new power. This is more than a simple victory through increased strength: NERV saves headquarters but is even less certain what it controls.

**Issues:** Conditions for choosing again, uncontrolled power, and secondary consequences of victory.

### Episode 20 | The Shape of the Heart, the Shape of a Person

After Unit-01 awakens, Shinji's body no longer exists in its usual form inside the cockpit. Ritsuko and others attempt to recover him while technical procedures intertwine with his memories, desires, and self-understanding. He eventually returns to reality, and those close to him recognize that this was more than a mechanical fault. Kaji leaves further clues with Misato as the intelligence investigation approaches NERV's core.

**Issues:** Continuity of identity, technical limits of rescue, and the wish to return to reality.

### Episode 21 | The Birth of NERV

SEELE's distrust of Gendo becomes explicit, and Fuyutsuki is taken away for questioning. Flashbacks connect his encounters with Yui and Gendo, the organization's formation after Second Impact, and Naoko's history with MAGI. Kaji rescues Fuyutsuki but pays for his intelligence work with his life; Misato receives his final message. The episode supplies background while showing that people recount history through their feelings and positions.

**Issues:** Institutional origins, designers' intentions, and the difference between testimony and records.

### Episode 22 | At Least, Be Human

Asuka's synchronization and confidence decline as earlier trauma intrudes on the present. An Angel attacks her psychologically from above and forces her to relive painful memories on the battlefield. Gendo orders Rei to kill it using the Spear of Longinus. Asuka feels replaced and worthless rather than rescued. A military victory does not automatically heal an injured pilot.

**Issues:** Psychological attacks, performance and self-worth, and care after rescue.

### Episode 23 | Tears

Asuka can barely fight. Rei confronts a new Angel in Unit-00 and suffers physical and psychological invasion. She sacrifices Unit-00 to protect Shinji. A subsequent “Rei” makes Shinji realize events do not follow ordinary expectations about death and survival. Ritsuko breaks with Gendo, shows Misato and Shinji the hidden Rei clone facility, and destroys it. Questions about Rei's identity become unavoidable facts rather than hints.

**Issues:** Self-sacrifice, cloning and personal identity, and the cost of concealment.

### Episode 24 | The Final Messenger

After losing important relationships and enduring repeated blows, Shinji meets Kaworu Nagisa and quickly feels understood. Kaworu reveals himself as an Angel, controls Unit-02, and descends underground. There he finds that the target is not what he expected. To prevent catastrophe, Kaworu leaves the choice of life and death to Shinji, who kills him. The military crisis ends while Shinji moves closer to emotional collapse.

**Issues:** Trust and deception, trauma from an unavoidable choice, and the enemy's personhood.

### Episode 25 | The World Coming to an End

The TV ending turns inward to the characters' consciousness. Shinji, Asuka, Rei, Misato, and others narrate and question one another, asking why they seek recognition yet fear being seen. Instrumentality is experienced as changes in boundaries of consciousness. Concrete external actions are not fully depicted in conventional battle narrative. Read this episode as psychological and conceptual material, not a basis for inventing a battlefield record that was never shown.

**Issues:** Narratives of the self, dependence and rejection, and subjective experiences of Instrumentality.

### Episode 26 | The Beast That Shouted Love at the Heart of the World

Shinji continues confronting whether he has worth beyond piloting EVA. Different situations and forms of expression show that self-understanding is neither singular nor fixed and that the world is more than one's own fears. Shinji begins accepting other possibilities for himself. The TV ending concludes with a change in his internal judgment, establishing a psychological turning point without explaining every subsequent external event.

**Issues:** Self-worth, possibilities for another life, and an open external ending.

## EoE: The Film Ending

The film begins after TV episode 24. Interpretations of its relationship to the final two TV episodes differ; the two presentations remain separate here.

**25′, Air:** The story continues after TV episode 24 [3]. Feeling guilty about killing Kaworu Nagisa, Shinji Ikari visits Asuka's hospital room seeking comfort, but the unconscious Asuka cannot respond. Shinji masturbates in front of her and subsequently sinks into self-loathing.

After the last Angel, Kaworu, is eliminated, SEELE launches an all-out attack on the NERV headquarters controlled by Gendo Ikari to advance its own Human Instrumentality Project. It first directs other countries to use backup MAGI systems to hack NERV's MAGI-01, but Ritsuko Akagi blocks the intrusion with defense program 666. SEELE then induces the Japanese government to send the Strategic Self-Defense Force into the underground city to kill NERV personnel. Knowing the truth about Instrumentality, Misato Katsuragi orders the EVA pilots' safety secured: Asuka is placed inside Unit-02 and hidden in the underground lake. Shinji, wandering alone, falls into danger and is rescued by Misato. The Strategic Self-Defense Force bombards the lake with an N² weapon and numerous depth charges. Terrified by the attack, Asuka recognizes her mother's presence, regains her resolve, and pilots Unit-02 to destroy the attackers' weapons. SEELE deploys nine mass-production EVAs against Unit-02. (The REBIRTH segment ends here.)

Mortally wounded, Misato urges Shinji to pilot Unit-01 again and sends him by elevator to its hangar, but resin seals the EVA and prevents entry. Gendo brings Rei Ayanami to Terminal Dogma, where Lilith is located. Ritsuko, waiting there, attempts to activate NERV's self-destruct program, but MAGI rejects the decision; Gendo then shoots and kills her. Meanwhile, Asuka defeats the mass-production EVAs one after another. Just as she appears close to destroying them all, a replica Spear of Longinus pierces Unit-02's head and it stops operating. The mass-production EVAs revive and tear Unit-02 apart. At that moment, seemingly responding to Shinji, Unit-01 breaks out of its restraints on its own. Shinji can finally launch, only to see Unit-02's dismembered remains.

**26′, My Purest Heart for You:**
Gendo places his right hand, into which Adam has been transplanted, inside Rei's body. He attempts the forbidden fusion of Adam and Lilith to unite all souls, treating it as the only means of achieving his ultimate goal: reuniting with Yui Ikari.
Seeing Unit-02's remains, Shinji breaks down. Unit-01 spreads crimson cross-shaped wings and recalls the Spear of Longinus from the Moon. SEELE then begins the Instrumentality ritual using Unit-01 as its medium. The airborne mass-production EVAs and Unit-01 form the pattern of the Kabbalistic Tree of Life, triggering Third Impact. Rei rejects Gendo, absorbs Adam from his right hand, and returns to Lilith's body. Lilith takes on Rei's face, grows to an immense size, and appears before Shinji. The Spear of Longinus enters Unit-01's core, transforming it into the Tree of Life, a symbol of life's embryo. Instrumentality begins. Lilith appears beside people as an apparition of Rei, then takes the form of the person each most wishes to see. Humanity, feeling fulfilled, returns to LCL, and all souls merge into Lilith. Speaking with Kaworu and Rei in the world where everyone has become one, Shinji wishes to see everyone again. Instrumentality stops, Lilith disintegrates, and the human souls within her return to Earth. Anyone who can envision their own form in their heart can regain human form.
Regaining awareness on a beach, Shinji finds only Asuka beside him. He suddenly grips her throat. Asuka keeps looking expressionlessly at the sky without resisting, then reaches up to touch his face. Shinji releases his grip and cries. As he weeps, she looks toward him and quietly says, “Disgusting” [Note 2].

## Plot Landmarks and Terms

- System intrusion and human countermeasures: TV 13. Automatic control overriding a pilot's refusal: TV 18. MAGI refusing self-destruction: EoE 25′. Their permissions and circumstances differ.
- Organizational secrets emerge gradually: TV 01–12 primarily show defense missions; TV 13–20 expose anomalies in systems and EVAs; TV 21–24 reveal organizational history and identities; TV 25–26 and EoE depict Instrumentality. Early characters do not foresee later secrets.
- “EVA / Evangelion” and alternative translations for Unit-02 refer to the same entities. Synchronization rate describes a pilot–EVA connection, not a person's worth. The Dummy System refers to the control mechanism that attacks against Shinji's wishes in TV 18.
